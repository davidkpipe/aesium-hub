Each file is deliberately short: a starting point to expand with the developer, not a finished spec. Section numbers (§) refer to the source document.

How to read this

The requirements draw a line between two kinds of thing:

  • Foundations are shared building blocks. Each is built once and used by several features (CMS, accounts, publishing, tagging, media, commerce, payments, admin, hosting, analytics, search).
  • Content types are the features the audience sees (artist profiles, mixes, series, editorial, videos, events, products). Each is a record in the CMS that relies on the foundations rather than being its own system.

The entity diagram shows how the records relate. The statement of work prices the build, phases and hosting options.

Foundations

# Foundation One-liner
01 Accounts & Permissions Free audience accounts, staff roles. Artists have no accounts in V1.
02 CMS & Content Model The records and, crucially, the relationships between them. Shared hub structure for Artist Profiles and Series.
03 Publishing Workflow Draft, scheduled, published, unpublished. Featured and homepage placement.
04 Tagging & Classification Managed genre list (multi-select on mixes) plus existing fixed classifications.
05 Media Assets Images and artwork for every record.
06 Music Upload & Audio Hosting Getting mix audio in and delivered. Gated by licensing and the provider decision.
07 Audio Player Persistent Now Playing bar, full-view player, volume. Shapes the whole site's navigation.
08 Video Upload & Hosting Video provider, playback, rights.
09 Audience Activity Follow, like, save. Private, with public aggregate follower counts.
10 Notifications Preference-driven updates from followed artists.
11 Commerce & Cart Products, cart, checkout model, commission tracking.
12 Payments Processing, refunds, reconciliation, artist payouts.
13 Admin / Back Office One place for staff to manage everything and the links between records.
14 Content Submission Artist → Aesium review → publish. Manual or structured form.
15 Search & Discovery Keyword search and genre filtering over the existing content model.
16 Analytics Web, plays, views, activity, commerce and events made useful together.
17 Hosting & Infrastructure App, database, assets, backups, monitoring. Decided last.

Content types

Content type One-liner
Artist Profiles Hub page per artist. Aesium-managed. Follow and follower count.
Mixes Core audio content. Artist, optional Series episode, genres, like/save.
Series Theme-led hub collecting mixes (ordered episodes), video, editorial, shop content, links.
Editorial / Articles Interviews and articles linked to artists and/or Series.
Videos Behind the Scenes, Live Sessions, Music Videos, Event Recordings.
Events Upcoming / past, Next Up, ticket or RSVP action, recordings.
Products / Shop Artist-attributed products, Aesium takes a percentage, no stock handling.

Notes on the matrix:

  • Artist Profiles need Tagging because related artists are derived from the genres on an artist's mixes (§7).
  • Videos and Products use their own fixed classifications (Video Category, Shop Category) rather than genre (§4).
  • Events touch Commerce and Payments only if Aesium hosts ticket checkout (§11, §15).
  • The Audio Player affects Hosting because playback must survive page navigation (§5, §17, §20).

Out of scope for V1

  • Membership / paywall. Audience accounts stay free (§1).
  • Artist self-service accounts (§7).
  • Cross-visit persistence for player position, volume and cart (§5, §6, §13).
  • Queue / playlist / autoplay (§5).
  • Tagging beyond genre on mixes; mood, BPM, region; recommendations (§4).
  • Artist-facing analytics (§18).
